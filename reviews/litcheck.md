### (1) Verdict: SOLVED 
**SOLVED (with constraints).** The classification of which wallpaper and space groups can be realized by monochromatic laser interference has been fully established, primarily in the *interference/holographic lithography* literature. Because interfering monochromatic beams restrict all generating reciprocal lattice vectors to the surface of a sphere ($|\mathbf{k}_i| = k_0$, so wavevectors are $\Delta\mathbf{k} = \mathbf{k}_i - \mathbf{k}_j$), it is algebraically and geometrically impossible to realize all spatial symmetries in a single exposure. It has been proven that only **9 of the 17 2D wallpaper groups** can be formed via a single monochromatic exposure; achieving the other 8—along with a large fraction of the **230 3D space groups** (especially those featuring specific non-symmorphic glide planes or screw axes)—strictly requires breaking the monochromatic constraint via multiple exposures, relative phase shifts, or multiple wavelengths.

### (2) & (3) Most Relevant Papers (Authors / Year / Venue) and What They Showed

**On the limits of optical-lattice/interference space groups:**
*   **Cai, L. Z., Yang, X. L., & Wang, Y. R.** (2005). *Journal of the Optical Society of America A* (JOSA A). 
    *   *What they showed:* Systematically mapped the crystallographic limits of single-exposure monochromatic interference in 2D, proving that exactly 9 of the 17 wallpaper groups can be physically formed, while the remaining 8 rigorously require multiple exposures or phase-shift techniques.
*   **Ullal, C. K., Maldovan, M., & Thomas, E. L.** (2003). *Journal of the Optical Society of America A* (JOSA A). 
    *   *What they showed:* Developed a general level-set mathematical framework mapping 3D monochromatic beam parameters to the 230 space groups, formally identifying which 3D periodic symmetries (e.g., chiral gyroid or diamond structures) can and cannot be achieved without multiple exposures.

**On optical quasicrystals (Pentagonal, Eightfold, and Icosahedral):**
*   **Guidoni, L., Trénec, C., Verkerk, P., & Grynberg, G.** (1997). *Physical Review Letters*.
    *   *What they showed:* Pioneered the field of optical quasicrystals by experimentally creating a 2D pentagonal (5-fold) quasiperiodic intensity landscape with five interfering laser beams, using it to trap ultracold atoms.
*   **Viebahn, K., Sbroscia, M., Carter, E., Yu, J. C., & Schneider, U.** (2019). *Physical Review Letters*.
    *   *What they showed:* Realized an eight-fold symmetric (octagonal) 2D optical quasicrystal using four intersecting standing waves, and utilized it to observe interaction-dependent matter-wave localization. 
*   **Lai, N. D., Liang, W. P., Lin, J. H., Hsu, C. C., & Lin, C. H.** (2007). *Optics Express*.
    *   *What they showed:* Theoretically proposed and experimentally achieved a full 3D icosahedral optical quasicrystal structure using a novel, highly symmetric 7-beam monochromatic interference setup.

**On the Fourier module rank criterion (Periodic vs. Quasiperiodic):**
*   **Lifshitz, R.** (2007). *Zeitschrift für Kristallographie - Crystalline Materials*. 
    *   *What they showed:* Clarified the rigorous modern definition of aperiodic order, demonstrating that a continuous density profile (such as an intensity potential made by a superposition of cosines) is *periodic* if the integer rank $N$ of its generated Fourier module equals the spatial dimension $D$, and is *quasiperiodic* if the rank exceeds the dimension ($N > D$).
